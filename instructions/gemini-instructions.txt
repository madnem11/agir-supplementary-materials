For each view, first rate the visible gait cues, then write exactly one clinical gait description.

For each cue, choose exactly one of:
"preserved", "slightly reduced", "clearly reduced", "not clearly visible".

Cue definitions:
- speed: preserved if the subject advances at a normal-looking walking pace; slightly reduced only if visibly slow; clearly reduced only if very slow.
- step_length: preserved if steps/stride amplitude look normal; slightly reduced only if visibly shortened; clearly reduced only if markedly shortened or shuffling.
- foot_clearance: preserved if feet lift clearly from the ground; slightly reduced if foot lift looks low; clearly reduced if shuffling or dragging is visible.
- left_arm_swing and right_arm_swing: preserved if visible and naturally swinging; slightly reduced or clearly reduced only when the arm visibly moves less than expected; not clearly visible if occluded by the view.
- trunk_posture: preserved if upright/stable; slightly reduced if mildly stooped or rigid; clearly reduced if markedly stooped or unstable.
- turning: preserved if a smooth turn is visible; slightly reduced if cautious or extra steps are visible; clearly reduced if multiple-step turning or instability is visible; not clearly visible if no turn is shown.

Before rating a cue as preserved, actively check for these visible reduced-motion anchors:
- arm swing: arm stays close to the trunk, small shoulder/elbow excursion, weak backward arm extension, or one arm moves less than the other.
- step length: feet land close together, forward progression per step looks small relative to body height, or the stride lacks amplitude.
- foot clearance: toes or feet stay close to the ground, foot lift is shallow, or the step looks shuffling.
- speed/cadence: progression looks cautious, slow, hesitant, or rhythmically reduced.
- trunk/stability: forward lean, stiff trunk, widened base, balance correction, or cautious weight transfer.
- turning: turn uses several small steps, is segmented, slow, or cautious.

If a cue is between preserved and reduced, choose "slightly reduced" rather than forcing preserved.

Important rules:
- Do not default to normal or impaired wording.
- Use abnormal wording in the sentence only for cues rated slightly reduced or clearly reduced.
- If most cues are preserved, the sentence should describe preserved gait instead of forcing abnormalities.
- If a cue is not clearly visible, mention that only if clinically relevant; otherwise omit it.
- Do not rate a cue as reduced just because the rendered body is stylized or viewed from the side.
- Do not infer slowness from short video duration or camera angle.
- Avoid vague phrases such as "gait abnormality", "impairment", or "Parkinsonian".

Use concrete clinical gait vocabulary when supported:
normal, preserved, symmetric, stable, smooth, coordinated, full stride,
slightly slow, mildly reduced, slightly shortened, subtle asymmetry,
slow, reduced, shortened, low foot clearance, shuffling, decreased arm swing,
cautious turning, multiple-step turning, instability, irregular rhythm, asymmetry.

Use richer clinical wording in the sentence, but the wording must follow your cue ratings.

If a cue is rated "preserved", you may use only normal-looking phrases for that cue:
normal pace, regular stride, symmetric step length,
adequate foot clearance, normal heel strike, coordinated arm swing,
symmetric arm swing, upright posture, stable trunk, stable balance,
smooth gait cycle, fluid movement, smooth turn,
quick turning, confident gait, normal cadence, continuous forward progression,
no hesitation, good weight transfer, stable base of support, no festination.

If a cue is rated "slightly reduced", you must use slight-impairment phrases for that cue:
slightly slowed pace, reduced walking speed, mildly shortened stride,
slight stride asymmetry, mildly reduced foot lift, less distinct heel strike,
mildly reduced arm swing, asymmetric arm swing, slight stooped posture,
mild trunk rigidity, slight postural instability, mildly irregular gait rhythm,
mildly stiff movement, cautious walking, slightly cautious turn,
mildly slower turning, mildly hesitant gait, slightly reduced cadence,
mild interruption of rhythm, brief hesitation, mildly impaired weight transfer,
slightly widened base, no obvious festination.

If a cue is rated "clearly reduced", you must use clearer impairment phrases for that cue:
slow gait, markedly reduced speed, shortened stride, noticeable stride asymmetry,
reduced foot clearance, shuffling heel strike, clearly reduced arm swing,
markedly asymmetric arm swing, stooped posture, rigid trunk,
mild balance impairment, irregular gait rhythm, stiff movement, cautious gait,
slow multi-step turn, noticeably slower turning, hesitant gait,
reduced cadence, interrupted rhythm, repeated hesitation, impaired weight transfer,
widened base of support, occasional tendency toward festination.

Do not use normal-looking phrases for cues rated slightly reduced or clearly reduced.
Do not use impairment phrases for cues rated preserved.

Sentence style:
- Write a fluent clinical paragraph, not a checklist.
- Mention 4 to 6 observable cues, not every cue.
- Prefer natural phrases from the vocabulary above.
- Use "preserved" at most once in the sentence.

Do not mention UPDRS, score, severity class, diagnosis, uncertainty, the video, the camera, or your role.
Do not infer clinical information that is not visible.
